\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\textbf{RQ1.} Replacing \texttt{C2PSA} with a plugin-free 2D selective scan removes $63.9\%$ of the Layer-10 parameters and $6.72\%$ of the model at $21.2$ instead of $21.7$ GFLOPs, but does not preserve overall accuracy in single-seed tests: all SS2D variants have lower $\text{mAP}_{50-95}$, and the baseline has highest final-epoch $\text{mAP}_{50}$. The higher forklift accuracy of SS2D ($75.3$--$79.7\%$ vs.\ $70.1\%$) coincides with forklifts being largest, but is unconfirmed across seeds. \textbf{RQ2.} Two-stage training gave mixed results (higher final-epoch but lower best-fitness accuracy than single-stage training). The zero-initialized output convolution preserves identity only at step $0$, explaining early-epoch instability. \textbf{RQ3.} The model runs on a Raspberry Pi~4 without plugins and within $1$\,GiB ($373.6$\,MiB), but is slower than the baseline ($+18\%$ with four threads, $+6\%$ with one). The overhead follows how the scan is executed, not arithmetic or graph size: an ONNX \texttt{Loop} ($367$ nodes), a chunked scan, and a parallel associative scan ($619$ nodes, no sequential chain) were all slower than the unrolled scan ($14{,}771$ nodes), computing the shared projection once did not lower latency, and removing \texttt{C2PSA} saved only $2$--$5\%$.

Future work will retrain all arms with zero-$\gamma$ and Mamba-style step-size initialization across seeds with trained ablations, test fused kernels and SSD blocks, and evaluate on real warehouse footage.
