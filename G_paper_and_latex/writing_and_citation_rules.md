# Academic Writing and Citation Rules

## 1. Research integrity

- Never fabricate a source, result, citation, dataset statistic, or experiment.
- Never write a planned result as if it has been measured.
- Distinguish prior-work results from this project's reproduced results.
- Do not claim novelty without reviewing the closest competing methods.
- Do not claim statistical significance without an appropriate analysis.
- Do not hide negative results that materially affect the conclusion.

## 2. Claim types

Mark claims during drafting:
- `[SOURCE]` prior-work or dataset fact.
- `[MEASURED]` project result.
- `[DERIVED]` calculation from recorded values.
- `[HYPOTHESIS]` expectation to test.
- `[PROPOSAL]` planned method.
- `[TODO]` unresolved claim.

Remove internal labels from the final paper only after every claim has been verified.

## 3. Paraphrasing

- Write in your own words.
- Cite the source of the idea or factual claim.
- Do not copy long passages from papers.
- Preserve technical meaning when paraphrasing.
- Quote only when necessary and follow copyright/venue rules.
- Do not rely on an abstract for a detailed architecture claim when the full paper is available.

## 4. Quantitative claims

Every number must have:
- a source or experiment ID;
- a clear unit;
- a measurement context;
- a definition of the metric;
- enough detail to interpret the comparison.

For derived percentages, retain the formula and input values in the experiment record.

## 5. Novelty language

Avoid unsupported phrases:
- “the first”
- “the only”
- “no existing work”
- “state-of-the-art”
- “significantly better”
- “real-time” without a measured target/protocol.

Prefer bounded statements:
- “To the best of our knowledge, within the reviewed literature and search scope…”
- “In the experiments conducted under the stated protocol…”
- “The results suggest…”
- “The method is evaluated on…”
- “We investigate whether…”

Even qualified wording requires a credible literature search.

## 6. Method transparency

Report:
- the exact architecture used;
- changes relative to the closest prior method;
- data preparation and split rules;
- training settings;
- evaluation settings;
- hardware/runtime;
- known limitations.

## 7. Discussion discipline

Separate:
- observation: what the numbers/images show;
- interpretation: a plausible explanation;
- causal claim: what the experiment can genuinely isolate;
- limitation: what cannot be concluded.

## 8. AI-assisted writing

AI may help organize, edit, summarize, and draft, but:
- verify every citation against the source;
- inspect original papers before relying on technical claims;
- do not invent source details;
- follow the target venue's AI-use/disclosure policy;
- keep authors responsible for correctness and originality.
